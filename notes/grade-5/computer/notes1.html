CH-7 {E-MAIL AND MALWARE SAFETY}

SNAIL MAIL

The system of sending and receiving physical letters through postal services.

WHAT IS E-MAIL?

E-mail stands for Electronic Mail. It is used to send and receive messages electronically over the Internet. E-mails can be sent to people in different places very quickly.

PARTS OF AN E-MAIL

Compose – Create a new e-mail.

From – The sender's e-mail address.

To – The e-mail address of the main recipient or recipients.

Subject – A brief title or topic of the e-mail.

CC – Carbon Copy. It is used to send a copy of the e-mail to additional recipients.

BCC – Blind Carbon Copy. It is used to send a copy of the e-mail without showing the BCC recipients to other recipients.

Message – The main content or text of the e-mail.

Attachment – A file attached to an e-mail, such as an image, document, video, or PDF.

Send – Send the e-mail to the recipient(s).

Reply – Respond to an e-mail that has been received.

Forward – Send a received e-mail to another person.

E-MAIL FOLDERS

Inbox – Contains e-mails that have been received.

Sent – Contains e-mails that have been sent.

Drafts – Contains e-mails that have been written but saved to be sent later.

Spam – Contains unwanted or unsolicited e-mails, often sent in large numbers.

MALWARE

Malware stands for Malicious Software. It is software designed to harm a computer, steal information, or perform other unwanted activities.

TYPES OF MALWARE

Virus – A malicious program that can attach itself to files and spread when the infected file is opened.

Worm – Malware that can spread automatically from one computer to another, often through a network.

Trojan Horse – A malicious program that pretends to be legitimate or useful software.

Spyware – Malware that secretly collects information about a user or their activities.

Ransomware – Malware that locks or encrypts files and demands payment from the victim.

E-MAIL AND MALWARE SAFETY

  • Do not open attachments from unknown or suspicious senders.
  • Do not click on suspicious links in e-mails.
  • Check the sender's e-mail address carefully.
  • Do not share passwords or other personal information through suspicious e-mails.
  • Use reliable antivirus or security software.
  • Keep the operating system and software updated.
  • Use strong and unique passwords.
  • Be careful of e-mails asking for urgent payments or personal information.
  • If an e-mail looks suspicious, delete it or report it as spam.
